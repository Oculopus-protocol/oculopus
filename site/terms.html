These terms cover the Oculopus website, the public node API, and the paid reputation oracle (together, the Service). Using any of them means you accept them.

1. What Oculopus is

Oculopus reads public data from Arc and turns settled USDC payments into signed receipts, then computes a reputation score for the parties to those payments. Every input is already public on a blockchain, and the scoring function is open source, so anyone can recompute any score themselves. A runner for doing exactly that is published at Oculopus-protocol/node.

Oculopus deploys no smart contract of its own. It issues no token. It operates no exchange and runs no lending market.

2. What a score is — and what it is not

A score is an automated statistical estimate computed from public blockchain records by a published formula. It is not a statement of fact about any person or business, not a credit rating in any regulated sense, and not financial, investment, legal or tax advice.

Specific limits you should understand before relying on one:

  • A score reflects only evidence Oculopus has indexed. Activity on other chains, off-chain dealings and anything outside the receipt format are invisible to it.
  • Evidence decays on a 14-day half-life, so a score describes recent behaviour. A high score is not a prediction, and a low score is not an accusation.
  • A new address scores 25 because it has no history, not because anything is wrong with it. Absence of evidence is not evidence of bad conduct.
  • No scoring model is immune to manipulation. Oculopus publishes its anti-gaming measures and their known limits in the documentation, and you should read them before treating a score as decisive.

Any decision you take after reading a score — extending credit, hiring an agent, routing a payment, declining to — is your decision, taken on your own assessment. Oculopus is one input, never the whole answer.

If you believe a score about you is wrong, write to contact@oculopus.xyz. Because the formula and the inputs are both public, a disagreement about a score is usually resolvable by recomputing it, and we would rather hear about it than not.

3. Oculopus never holds your assets

The Service takes no custody of funds, tokens or private keys. Signing in uses a wallet signature to prove address ownership; it never asks for or receives a private key or a seed phrase, and no part of Oculopus will ever ask you for one. Payments for the paid oracle settle directly between your wallet and the Oculopus treasury through Circle Gateway. You remain in control of your own wallet at all times, and you are responsible for its security.

4. API access and keys

Free reads from the public node are offered as a public good, subject to rate limits. The paid oracle is metered. An API key you create is bound to the wallet that created it.

  • Treat a key like a password. You are responsible for calls made with your key, including calls you did not intend; revoke a key immediately if it is exposed.
  • Free allowances are per wallet, not per key. Creating more keys does not create more allowance, and attempting to multiply an allowance by creating wallets is a breach of section 6.
  • Rate limits and monthly allowances are published on the API page and may be adjusted. We will not reduce a paid allowance you have already bought within its paid period.
  • We may suspend a key or a wallet that is breaking these terms, and will say why.

5. Payments

Paid calls settle in USDC over the x402 protocol through Circle Gateway, on-chain and in real time. By their nature those settlements are final and non-reversible: there is no mechanism for Oculopus to reverse one, and paid calls are non-refundable except where refusing a refund would be unlawful.

You are responsible for any taxes arising on your side of a transaction. Prices are quoted in USDC and may change; a change applies to calls made after it is published.

6. Acceptable use

Do not:

  • Manufacture evidence. Creating receipts for payments that are not real commercial activity — self-dealing between wallets you control, wash trading, or coordinating with others to inflate or deflate a score — is the one abuse that damages every other user of the Service.
  • Attack availability. No attempt to exceed, evade or circumvent rate limits, and no deliberate load intended to degrade the Service for others.
  • Misrepresent a score. Do not present an Oculopus score as a regulated credit rating, as a guarantee, or as a verification of identity, licensing or solvency.
  • Break the law with it. No use of the Service for unlawful purposes, and no use that would breach sanctions applicable to you or to us.
  • Impersonate. Do not claim to be Oculopus, or to be endorsed or certified by it.

Reading, copying, recomputing, republishing and criticising our scores is expressly permitted. A reputation system nobody can check is not worth having.

7. Changes to the scoring formula

The scoring model will change as we find weaknesses in it — we have already changed it to close one — and a change can move published scores, sometimes materially. We publish what changed and why. Do not build anything that assumes a score is stable, and do not treat a past score as a commitment about a future one.

We may also change these terms. The effective date at the top always shows the current version; continuing to use the Service after a change means you accept it.

10. Disputes, and how to reach us

If something goes wrong, tell us first.
