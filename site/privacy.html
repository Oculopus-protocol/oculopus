There is no account to create, no email address to give us, no password, and no advertising. The site sets no cookies and runs no analytics and no third-party scripts. You can verify every word of that by reading the page source, which is why we put it first.

1. Public blockchain data is not collected from you

Most of what Oculopus shows — addresses, payments, receipts, scores — comes from Arc, a public blockchain. That data was published by the people who made those transactions, is readable by anyone running a node, and we did not obtain it from you.

Two consequences worth being direct about. First, we cannot delete it: a blockchain record is not ours to erase, and removing an address from our index does not remove it from Arc. Second, a wallet address is pseudonymous, not anonymous — if an address is linked to you somewhere else, activity we display can be linked to you too. That is a property of public blockchains generally, not of Oculopus, but you should understand it before publishing receipts.

2. What we actually store about you

Only what the Service cannot work without:

WhatWhenWhy
Wallet addressWhen you sign in to the API dashboard To bind API keys to their owner so nobody else can list or revoke them
API keys you createWhen you create one To recognise your key and meter it
Call counts per monthAs you use the API To apply your free allowance and determine next month's tier
Session tokenAt sign-in A signed token holding only your address and an expiry. No database session
IP addressOn every request Rate limiting, briefly — see below

We do not ask for or hold your name, email address, phone number, date of birth, postal address, government ID or payment card. We never receive your private key or seed phrase, and no part of Oculopus will ever ask you for one.

About IP addresses

Rate limiting needs to tell callers apart. An unauthenticated request is counted against its IP, held in memory for as long as that caller stays active and discarded about ten minutes after they stop. Our web server also writes standard access logs, which include IP addresses, for security and debugging. We do not build profiles from them, do not combine them with your wallet address, and do not share them for advertising.

3. What stays on your device

The site uses browser local storage, not cookies. Three small values, all first-party, none transmitted to us as part of a tracking scheme:

  • oc-token — your session token, so a page reload does not sign you out.
  • oc-addr — the wallet address you signed in with, to show it in the interface.
  • oc-api — which API endpoint the page should call.

Clearing your browser's site data removes all three and signs you out. Because none of this is a cookie and none of it is used for tracking or advertising, there is no consent banner to click — we would rather not store anything than ask you to dismiss a dialog.

4. Why we process it, and on what basis

Where the GDPR or a similar law applies to you, our basis is legitimate interest: running a metered API, applying allowances fairly, and keeping a public endpoint available against abuse. We have kept what we hold to the minimum that makes those possible. We do not process special categories of data, do not use your data for automated decisions about you as a person, and do not sell data to anyone.

The reputation score is an automated computation about a wallet address, from public records, by a published formula you can run yourself. The Terms of Use set out what that score is and is not.

6. How long we keep it

  • Rate-limit records — in memory, discarded roughly ten minutes after you stop making requests.
  • Access logs — rotated by the server; we do not archive them long term.
  • API keys — until you revoke them, or you ask us to delete your account data.
  • Usage counts — kept per month, because next month's free allowance depends on last month's paid usage.
  • Session tokens — not stored by us at all. A token is signed, carries its own expiry, and we verify it without a lookup. Signing out everywhere invalidates every token issued to your address before that moment.

7. Your rights

You can do most of this yourself, immediately, without asking us:

  • See it — the dashboard shows every key your wallet owns and its usage.
  • Delete it — revoke your keys; sign out everywhere to kill your sessions; clear site data to remove local storage.
  • Stop — stop using the Service. There is no account left behind, because there was never an account.

For anything we cannot give you a button for — a copy of what we hold, deletion of your usage records, an objection to processing — write to contact@oculopus.xyz from, or alongside a signature from, the wallet concerned. A wallet signature is how we confirm an address is yours, since we hold no other identifier for you. What we cannot do is alter the blockchain, for the reason in section 1.

8. Contact and changes

  • Privacy requests and questions about this policy — contact@oculopus.xyz
  • Technical problems and security reports — support@oculopus.xyz

If this policy changes, the effective date above changes with it, and a change that materially increases what we collect will be announced on the site before it takes effect rather than after.
